The Water Cycle Lab

The water you drank today is very old. It has traveled from oceans to clouds to rivers and back again for millions of years. A dinosaur may have drunk some of it! In this lab you will learn how water moves around Earth. Then you will build your own water cycle and watch it come to life.

I Can Statements

    Station 1

    Where Is Earth's Water?

    I can explain why the ocean is an important part of the water cycle.

    Picture all of Earth's water as 100 squares. Almost all of it, 97 squares, is salty ocean water. About 2 squares are frozen in glaciers and ice caps. Only about 1 square is fresh liquid water in lakes, rivers, the ground, and the air. The water cycle connects all of these places.

    Ocean (salt water)Ice and glaciersLakes, rivers, groundwater, air

    Most of the water vapor in our air comes from the ocean. When ocean water evaporates, the salt stays behind. That is why rain is fresh water.

    Station 2

    Water Changes Its State

    I can explain how water changes between a solid, a liquid, and a gas.

    Water can be a solid (ice), a liquid (water), or a gas (water vapor). Adding heat energy makes the tiny particles move faster. Taking heat away slows them down. Slide the heat bar and watch the particles.

    Rain, Snow, Sleet, or Hail?

    I can tell the difference between rain, snow, sleet, and hail.

    Precipitation is any water that falls from clouds. The temperature of the air between the cloud and the ground decides what form it takes. Freezing is 32 degrees Fahrenheit.
